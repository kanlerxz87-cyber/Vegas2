Q-02 Transit Reality (Subway vs Monorail & Bus)

Does Las Vegas have a subway, and how many Michelin restaurants are truly within <=1 hour by public transit?

Verified Answer: Las Vegas has NO subway. Public transit is the Las Vegas Monorail (7 Strip stations, every 4–8 min; 7-day eTicket $59.95/adult = $119.90 for 2) plus RTC buses including the 24/7 Deuce on the Strip (3-day pass $20/adult = $40 for 2). From Center-Strip Monorail hotels (The LINQ, Flamingo, Horseshoe, Paris, Caesars Palace), 15 Michelin restaurants are directly on the Strip/Arts District transit corridor (13 Strip + 2 Arts District). The 6 Chinatown (Spring Mountain Rd) and 3 Off-Strip West/SW restaurants require an east–west RTC bus transfer (e.g., Route 203 / 202) or rideshare.
How to Discover Missing Info: Use the official RTC Southern Nevada Trip Planner (rtcsnv.com) with departure time 6:00 PM on a weekday from 'Las Vegas Blvd @ Flamingo' to each Chinatown/SW address (especially Anima by EDO at 9205 W Russell Rd and YEN Viet Kitchen at 6115 S Decatur Blvd) to confirm exact bus+walk minutes.

Q-05 Outdoor Parks Without a Car

Can we visit Red Rock Canyon, Hoover Dam, or Valley of Fire by bus/monorail or rideshare?

Verified Answer: NO for public transit — BLM explicitly confirms public transportation does not extend to Red Rock Canyon, and neither Hoover Dam nor Valley of Fire has Strip bus service. For Red Rock Canyon, BLM warns rideshare visitors are limited to the Visitor Center area; for Valley of Fire, Nevada State Parks explicitly warns Uber/Lyft can leave visitors stranded with no return ride. Renting a car for 1–2 days (or booking an authorized commercial tour) is the safe, reliable option.
How to Discover Missing Info: For Red Rock Canyon timed entry ($20 vehicle + $2 fee), log into Recreation.gov at 7:00 AM PT exactly 30 days before your chosen Feb 2027 date (e.g., Jan 18, 2027 for Feb 17). For Hoover Dam Guided Dam Tours ($40/pp, in-person only), arrive near 9:00 AM opening before tours sell out.
