Overview / Transit

Getting around: Monorail, buses and the 1-hour rule

What actually exists, what it reaches, and why this site refuses to print invented travel times.

There is no subway or metro in Las Vegas. The brief's "within 1 hour by subway or bus" test needs rewording: rail here means the 7-station Las Vegas Monorail, which never leaves the Strip. Everything else is RTC bus (including the Deuce on Las Vegas Blvd), walking, the free tram segments, rideshare or a car.

The trap for visitors: RTC's own page states that residential passes are valid on the SDX and Deuce only with a local I.D. Without Nevada ID you need the Strip/Downtown tier, which is why the Deuce costs $8 a day rather than the $5 residential figure often quoted.

Reduced fare (ages 6–17, seniors 60+, persons with disabilities, or a Medicare card, with photo ID): $1 single, $1.50 two-hour, $2.50 24-hour, $17 15-day, $32.50 30-day. Children five and under ride free with an adult.

The binding constraint is the Neon Museum, not the airport. On its own page it closes at 11 PM with last entry at 10 PM from September to May — which is your window — and the box office shuts an hour before closing. A late-afternoon landing makes this a next-day activity, not a first-night one.

The Conservatory has no published daily hours at all, only 2027 display dates, and it is dark 7–12 March. Because it is free and open to walk through, it is the one venue that survives a late arrival — but only on a date inside a show run.

Why there is no travel-time column

  • No official source publishes a hotel→restaurant journey time for a February 2027 date.
  • The RTC planner returns live results; capturing them would be a snapshot that expires, so it belongs in a dated log, not a static claim.
  • Converting distance into minutes would be an estimate — exactly what the brief forbids.

Practical consequence: for a restaurant-led trip, choose the hotel whose resort already contains the restaurants you want (Venetian: 4, MGM Grand: 2, Cosmopolitan: 2). That removes the transit question entirely.
